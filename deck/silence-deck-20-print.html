SIX FRAMES FROM THE SEARCH FOR ALIENS

The Great Silence Deck

20 cards: six frames for thinking about AI at civilisational scale, each with its exercise and its counter-argument.
They give you somewhere to stand while you think.
CARD01 / 20

The Fermi frame

THE FRAME

The universe is old and vast — where is everybody? Applied to systems: a system that says nothing is still telling you something, and reading that is the first move in thinking clearly about technology.

THE TEST

Silence forces a choice between three explanations — the model is wrong, the observation is incomplete, or something is actively hiding. Each points at a different action.

Absence is data.

CARD02 / 20

The counter-argument

THE FRAME

"You cannot monitor every absence. The combinatorics are impossible."

THE TEST

Correct — and irrelevant. The skill is not monitoring every absence; it is choosing which absences matter. Ask what would have to be true for the silence to be a problem. If you cannot answer, it is not a signal; it is just quiet. Over-monitoring absence produces alert fatigue, which is its own failure mode.

Choose which absences matter, then monitor them.

CARD03 / 20

The exercise

THE FRAME

Pick a system you use every day. List three things you would expect to see on a normal day and would notice only by their absence.

THE TEST

Then find out whether anyone actually monitors those absences. Most people cannot answer the second part.

Good operators treat absence as an event.

CARD04 / 20

The Liu Cixin frame

THE FRAME

The universe is hostile, and broadcasting your coordinates is fatal. Every organisation broadcasts. The question is never whether you leak, but who is listening and what they can infer.

THE TEST

Three leak classes: infrastructure (job ads, DNS, certificates), people (talks, profiles, commits), and AI (prompt logs, fine-tuning data, retrieval indexes — new broadcast surfaces that did not exist five years ago).

Attack surface is the sum of everything observable, not just the software.

CARD05 / 20

The counter-argument

THE FRAME

"You cannot run a modern organisation without broadcasting. Hiring, marketing, open-source contribution and documentation all require it."

THE TEST

Correct, and the frame does not say stop. It says choose. Most organisations broadcast by default and audit by accident. And it is no excuse for security through obscurity: invisible with no defences is safe until found.

Audit by default, not by accident.

CARD06 / 20

The exercise

THE FRAME

Audit your own public footprint as if you were a competitor or an attacker.

THE TEST

Check job ads, DNS records, certificate transparency logs, code activity, social posts and vendor status pages. Write down three things you did not realise you were broadcasting. Go further: harden a server until an external scan finds nothing.

One lit window in a dark forest is a signal. It is also a target.

CARD07 / 20

The thermodynamics frame

THE FRAME

Some civilisations might sleep, waiting for a colder universe where computation is cheaper. Heat is waste — and waste is a design decision made now, in architecture, in code, and in what we decide is worth running at all.

THE TEST

Erasing a bit has a minimum energy cost; real systems sit orders of magnitude above that floor. The gap between the physics and the engineering is the part you can act on.

Efficiency is a survival metric.

CARD08 / 20

The counter-argument

THE FRAME

"Renewable energy solves this" — and "more efficient models solve it themselves."

THE TEST

Renewables change the carbon cost, not the engineering cost; the GPU still draws power and the bill still arrives. And efficiency enables use: a model that costs half as much gets used twice as much. Jevons' paradox applies — the fix is better decisions about what is worth running.

Jevons' paradox: efficiency without decisions increases consumption.

CARD09 / 20

The exercise

THE FRAME

Take a script or notebook you already run.

THE TEST

Profile it for CPU time and memory. Find the single biggest inefficiency, fix it, and measure the difference. Report the saving as a number, not a feeling. Some computation is worth the energy — the frame asks you to know what you are spending and why.

Report the saving as a number, not a feeling.

CARD10 / 20

The observation frame

THE FRAME

Advanced civilisations observe without interfering. The line between watching and controlling is drawn by power, not by technology. Observability, analytics and algorithmic management are the same act seen from different sides.

THE TEST

A workplace that tracks keystrokes, breaks and bathroom visits is not measuring performance. It is performance. The same data is analytics in one room and surveillance in another — the technology does not decide which word applies; the relationship does.

The same data is analytics in one room and surveillance in another.

CARD11 / 20

The counter-argument

THE FRAME

"Observability is necessary. Without it you cannot debug, secure or improve a system."

THE TEST

True, and the frame does not argue against observability. It argues for knowing who is in the enclosure. A system where observation is invisible to the observed and feeds decisions they cannot see or appeal is a governance arrangement, not infrastructure.

Name who is in the enclosure and who holds the clipboard.

CARD12 / 20

The exercise

THE FRAME

Map every point where a product or workplace collects data about a person's behaviour.

THE TEST

For each, write down who can see the data, what decision it feeds, and whether the person it describes can see it too. The last column is usually the shortest. The frame applies to individual-level observation feeding decisions about individuals — anonymous aggregates are a different thing.

The last column is usually the shortest.

CARD13 / 20

The consensus frame

THE FRAME

Generals must agree while messages are lost and some are traitors. Consensus without a trusted authority is possible only under conditions most organisations refuse to meet — and the assumptions are the governance.

THE TEST

Known participants, bounded faults, message delivery. Change any assumption and the guarantee changes. If you cannot say how many participants may defect and still reach a decision, you do not have a governance model; you have a hope.

Name the assumptions or the governance is a hope.

CARD14 / 20

The counter-argument

THE FRAME

"Too theoretical. In practice someone senior reviews the output and catches the errors."

THE TEST

That works until the senior person is unavailable, the volume exceeds review capacity, or the senior person shares the blind spots. And a three-agent chain sharing one model is not three independent checks — it is one hallucination amplified through three voices.

Agents that share a model share failure modes.

CARD15 / 20

The exercise

THE FRAME

Map a real decision process you are part of.

THE TEST

Who are the participants? What counts as a faulty one? What happens if the faulty ones cannot be distinguished from the honest ones? Would the process survive one deliberate liar? The useful question is not "can we trust the participants?" but "how many can be wrong before the decision stops meaning anything?"

How many can be wrong before the decision stops meaning anything?

CARD16 / 20

The accountability frame

THE FRAME

Something prevents civilisations from reaching the stars. The most plausible filter is not an asteroid — it is a species that builds systems it cannot stop.

THE TEST

Capability without accountability is the risk: not malice, but competence without oversight. The alignment problem is the specification problem — a system optimising the wrong objective does not need to be malicious to be dangerous, only competent.

The filter is not an asteroid. It is a species that builds systems it cannot stop.

CARD17 / 20

The counter-argument

THE FRAME

"Alarmist — systems have off switches, and regulation is coming."

THE TEST

The question is not whether an off switch exists but who knows where it is and who has authority to use it. A kill switch nobody knows about is not a kill switch. And regulation operates after deployment, on a timescale of years; systems operate on a timescale of seconds. The gap is where the filter lives.

A kill switch nobody knows about is not a kill switch.

CARD18 / 20

The exercise

THE FRAME

Write a kill switch policy for one AI tool you actually use.

THE TEST

Name who can stop it, what triggers the stop, who is told, and what happens to work already in progress. If you cannot fill in those four fields, you have found the gap. Fail-safe design is older than AI — aviation, nuclear, medical — use it.

If you cannot fill in the four fields, you found the gap.

CARD19 / 20

Six frames, one argument

THE FRAME

Thinking about AI at civilisational scale collapses into either hype or doom, because both are positions rather than places to think.

THE TEST

Six frames from a mature literature on civilisational risk, each with an exercise, a counter-argument and a known failure mode. Absence is data. Broadcasts are readable. Heat is a decision. Observation is governance. Assumptions are the rule. The filter is us.

The frames do not tell you what to think.

CARD20 / 20

Somewhere to stand

THE FRAME

The industry asks worse questions than it could, because it has no vantage point outside itself.

THE TEST

Borrow the vantage point. Read the silence, audit the broadcast, price the heat, name the enclosure, test the assumptions, and ask who can turn it off.

They give you somewhere to stand while you think.
